International Art Magazine

I.A.M. interweaves international art interests with the local scene that feels them first — especially our home borough of Brooklyn. Below is the whole magazine, organized the way the Art Fairs, Museums, NYC, Artists, International and Culture sections are: a short introduction, then the articles, with notes pointing you toward related reading here and elsewhere.

New here? Start with the two most recent gallery walks — 10 Galleries in Chinatown and Ten Galleries in Tribeca to Visit in 2025 — then browse the NYC and Artists sections below.

Art Fairs

Reports from the fair circuit — Basel and Miami, the Armory Show, LA Art Week and the satellite fairs. Most of these link outward to the fairs themselves: Art Basel Miami Beach, The Armory Show, Frieze Los Angeles and SPRING/BREAK Art Show.

  • Miami Art Week 2023 Preview — two artists to catch at Art Basel and Untitled; pairs well with our earlier Good to Know.FYI at Art Basel report.
  • Getting to Know Good to Know.FYI at Art Basel — “PLEASURETOWN” — a curatorial collective punching above its weight amid the NFT-saturated fair.
  • LA Art Week 2022: Exploring the Art of LA — Frieze, SPRING/BREAK and the Felix Art Show in one week.
  • Microscope Gallery’s “Border Control” at the Armory Show — Peggy Ahwesh’s four-channel video at the San Diego–Tijuana border; Microscope is a Bushwick gallery.
  • When Does Supplemental Text Become Too Much? (SPRING/BREAK, Armory Week) — on wall text and press releases at fair booths and the New Museum Triennial.

For the New York end of fair week, see the NYC section — Satellite Art Show and No Vacancy III both run against Armory Week.

Museums

Museum shows and institutions, from MoMA to the Basilica Cistern in Istanbul. Several of these overlap with our International coverage.

  • Thoughts on Sarah Sze and the New MoMA — ten notes on Triple Point (Pendulum) and the reopened museum; see also Sze’s work at Tanya Bonakdar.
  • Stranger Vibes: Aurora Halal & Sam Rolfes, PopRally at MoMA — a late-night program with lighting by Nitemind; more on Nitemind in our Culture archive.
  • Fragility in Miami: Lauren Shapiro & Beatriz Chachamovits at the Jewish Museum — coral-reef loss, made during residencies at Bakehouse Art Complex.
  • Ancient and Contemporary Art Coalesce at the Basilica Cistern, Istanbul — showing new work inside a 1,500-year-old cistern; a companion piece to our International section.

NYC

The New York scene — neighborhood gallery guides, openings, and project spaces across the boroughs, with our home base along the East Williamsburg – Bushwick border. Artist-run spaces like ZXY Gallery and Paradice Palase recur throughout.

Neighborhood Guides

  • 10 Galleries in Chinatown — Lower Manhattan’s food district turns gallery district; features King’s Leap. Read alongside the Tribeca guide below.
  • Ten Galleries in Tribeca to Visit in 2025 — includes 15 Orient; a downtown counterpart to the Bushwick guide.
  • Bushwick: Where Does It Rank Among Centers for Art in NYC? — on gentrification, Luhring Augustine, C L E A R I N G, and the artist-run spaces that stayed.
  • Tonight in Long Island City — 550 Gallery and Radiator Gallery openings the same night.
  • Happening in Bushwick — Signal Gallery’s farewell and openings at The Border and Amos Eno; see the full BORDER piece and Irja Boden at Amos Eno.
  • Galleries We Love — the running list.

Spaces & Openings

  • No Vacancy III: The Power of Art in Brooklyn Culture — Alt Esc’s weekend-long exhibition, fair-sized, in an alternative space.
  • The BORDER, a New Project Space for Immigrant Art, Opens at 56 Bogart — artists from Slovakia, Iran/Greece, Peru, Georgia and Colombia; recurs in Happening in Bushwick.
  • The Bight at Honey Ramka: Works by Ben Finer — also covers Wendy Klemperer’s Salvage What You Can and Nitemind.
  • New Gallery in BK: Jorge Andrew Gallery — Southeast Williamsburg’s newest room; opens with Emma Stern. Cross-referenced from She Inspires.
  • Interactive Artwork “Hurrah!” Takes On NYCxDESIGN — a giant playable xylophone in Times Square, via NYCxDESIGN.

Shows & Reviews

  • Oscillator: Pentti Monkkonen Incorporates Sound at Jenny’s — a synthesizer sealed in a styrene cube; named an Artforum Must-See.
  • Worlds Collide at Backstory Universe by KIPNZ at Diana — Annie Hayes, Paul Latislaw and Rhys Ziemba at Diana New York, curated by KIPNZ.
  • Barbara Ess at Magenta Plains — VIEWING ROOM III: photography that unfixes its own certainty.
  • Thoughts on She Inspires at The Untitled Space — portraits of women who led the fight for equality; links onward to Jorge Andrew Gallery.
  • Show Review: Fogo! Group Exhibition at SoMad — 19 Latinx artists in support of Brazil’s Indigenous People Articulation (APIB).
  • Irja Boden’s Fractured Light at Amos Eno Gallery — stacked ceramic vessels; Amos Eno also appears in Happening in Bushwick.
  • Julie Speidel, “Continuities” at Winston Wächter — wood, bronze and stone benches meant to be sat on.
  • Byron Kim’s Sunday Paintings at James Cohan — 14×14 patches of sky with a diary entry written across each; also in Artists.

Fair-week overlap: Satellite Art Show and No Vacancy III both run during Armory Week — see the Art Fairs section.

Artists

Profiles, interviews and studio features on the artists we follow — from Bushwick founders to painters showing at David Zwirner and James Cohan.

  • Dean Cercone at Galerie Manqué — a founding member of Bushwick’s The Glove and Bohemian Grove who still lives there; context in the Bushwick guide.
  • Carlito Dalceggio — Interview — a nomadic multimedia artist in residence at Mana Contemporary, on words, color and concept.
  • The Process of Jovanni Luna at Paradice Palase — latex-paint “paintskins” and the platform Kat Ryals and Lauren Hirshfield built.
  • Jay Milder’s “Unblotting the Rainbow” in Provincetown — abstract expressionism, Jewish mysticism and the Kabbalah, curated by Adam Zucker at PAAM.
  • Byron Kim’s Sunday Paintings at James Cohan — a weekly sky, painted for years; cross-listed under NYC.
  • Portia Zvavahera at David Zwirner — the Zimbabwean painter’s first NYC solo; also in International.
  • Tara de la Garza — studio feature.
  • Pentti Monkkonen — Oscillator at Jenny’s — the Berlin-based artist’s first NYC solo show.
  • Irja Boden — Fractured Light — a ceramicist working method by method through one show.
  • Julie Speidel — “Continuities” — sculpture that doubles as furniture.

More artists appear inside the NYC reviews — Rebecca Ness, Emilie Stark-Menneg, Travis Boyer, Raymond Pettibon and Tim McCool among them.

International

Beyond New York — art and criticism with a global frame, and art-press politics that cross borders. Two of these also sit in Museums and Artists.

  • Ancient and Contemporary Art Coalesce at the Basilica Cistern, Istanbul — contemporary work by Ozan Ünal in a 6th-century Byzantine cistern.
  • Portia Zvavahera — dreams, liminality and large-scale painting from Harare to Chelsea.
  • Art Residencies: 25 Notable Programs East of Kansas — a working list for artists planning time away.
  • Art Press Challenging Colonialism, White Supremacy and Patriarchy — why Hyperallergic and ARTnews covering museum protests matters.
  • A Retort to Nietzsche’s “Will to Power” — a philosophical counter-argument.

Culture

Wine, spirits, food and travel — the interests that circle the art. Natural wine, Japan’s first vermouth, low-cost reds, and the LED work of Nitemind. Sub-topics: Liquor · Wine · Food · Travel.

  • The Awkward Quest for a Funky Natural Red Wine — what “natural” means when there’s no certification for it; see also RAW WINE.
  • Aesthetics Meets Taste: Japanese Bermutto — Oka Kura’s yuzu-kabosu-sansho-yomogi vermouth from a 140-year-old shochu distillery.
  • 3 Low-Cost, High-Value Red Wines — starting with Santa Rita’s 120 Carménère and the story behind the “120” name.
  • Wine — the full wine archive.
  • Liquor — spirits and cocktails, including tequila at Bossa Nova Civic Club.
  • Tasty Things — food notes.
  • Travel — where the art took us.

The line between Culture and the galleries is thin here — Nitemind’s lighting turns up at MoMA in the Museums section and at Honey Ramka in NYC.
